Chapter IIIHow blocking works

One night in the life of a tracker

A banner that only hides itself does nothing for privacy. cookieowl stops the browser from running trackers at all, and lets them in only after a choice.

  1. Dusk

    Hoot lights the lantern first

    Put the script tag at the top of <head>, without async or defer. Consent Mode defaults and the auto blocker are in place before anything else runs.

  2. Midnight

    Trackers wait outside as plain text

    Give each tracker type="text/plain" and a category. Browsers do not run text, so nothing is requested. Embeds keep their address in data-cookieowl-src.

  3. Morning

    A choice opens the gate

    On consent, each accepted script runs in document order, waiting for the one before it. Withdrawal sweeps out the category cookies and reloads the page.

Chapter IVWhat Hoot guards

Twelve promises Hoot keeps every night

Everything a consent banner should do, in one small file.

  1. Real blocking before consent

    Marked scripts and iframes stay inert, then run in document order once their category is accepted.

  2. Auto mode for known trackers

    Google Analytics, Tag Manager, Meta Pixel, Clarity, Hotjar, LinkedIn and TikTok are caught by URL, even when a snippet injects them.

  3. Google Consent Mode v2

    Denied defaults with wait_for_update, region defaults, an update on every choice and a dataLayer event for Tag Manager.

  4. Click to load embeds

    YouTube and Vimeo iframes become a placeholder with Load once and Allow marketing cookies.

  5. Categories and services

    Necessary, preferences, analytics, marketing and your own, each with services, cookies and durations.

  6. Records for audits

    Version, timestamp, random consent id and method in a first party cookie, plus an onConsent hook for your log.

  1. Withdraw as easily as giving

    A floating owl reopens the preferences anywhere, and withdrawal erases cookies and stops scripts.

  2. Privacy signals respected

    Global Privacy Control is honored by default, Do Not Track when you turn it on.

  3. Accessible, no dark patterns

    Focus trap, keyboard, screen reader labels, Reject as prominent as Accept and no pre-ticked boxes.

  4. Five languages, LGPD and GDPR

    Portuguese with LGPD wording, English, Spanish, French and German, RTL safe, every text overridable.

  5. Themed by one color

    Light and dark mode out of the box and a --seed color for the accent, in a scoped stylesheet.

  6. One small file

    About 15 kB gzip, no dependencies, no requests of its own, no account and no server.

Chapter VIIFAQ

Questions asked by lantern light

Not finding yours? Open an issue on GitHub.

Does cookieowl really block Google Analytics before consent?

Yes. Scripts you mark with type="text/plain" and data-cookieowl="analytics" are not executed by the browser at all, so gtag.js is never requested until analytics is accepted. With data-auto-block the gtag.js and Tag Manager URLs are also caught when they are inserted by a snippet. If you use Consent Mode advanced instead, Google tags load and send cookieless pings, which you choose explicitly.

Does it support Google Consent Mode v2?

Yes. With consentMode on, cookieowl sets ad_storage, ad_user_data, ad_personalization and analytics_storage to denied with wait_for_update before any tag runs, supports region specific defaults, and sends a consent update on every choice, including on later visits.

Is a cookie banner enough to comply with LGPD or GDPR?

No banner alone makes a site compliant. cookieowl handles the technical part: prior blocking, granular and free choice, withdrawal as easy as consent, versioned records and an audit hook. You still need an accurate privacy policy, a legal basis for each processing and a review by your own counsel. This site is not legal advice.

Where is the consent stored and for how long?

In a first party cookie named cookieowl (or localStorage if you prefer) holding the categories, a random consent id, the policy version, a timestamp and an expiry date. The default lifetime is 12 months, after which the banner asks again. Changing your policy version asks everyone again right away.

How do visitors change their mind?

A small floating owl button reopens the preferences on every page, and any element with data-cookieowl-open does the same, for example a Cookie settings link in your footer. Withdrawing erases the cookies of that category and reloads the page when its scripts already ran.

What happens with Global Privacy Control?

When a browser sends Global Privacy Control, cookieowl records a refusal of optional categories, does not show the banner and notes the signal in the preferences, where the visitor can still opt in. Do Not Track can be honored the same way with respectDNT.
