How blocking works

Three phases of the moon, from page load to consent

A banner that only hides itself does nothing for privacy. cookieowl stops the browser from running trackers in the first place, and only lets them in after a choice.

  1. Load cookieowl first

    Put the script tag at the top of <head>, without async or defer. Consent Mode defaults and the auto blocker are installed before anything else runs.

  2. Trackers wait as text

    Give each tracker type="text/plain" and a category. Browsers do not execute text, so nothing is requested. Embeds use data-cookieowl-src.

  3. A choice releases them

    On consent, each accepted script runs in document order, waiting for the one before it. Withdrawal erases the category cookies and reloads the page.

Auto mode knows 11 tracker patterns

Google Analytics, Google Tag Manager, Google Ads, Meta Pixel, Microsoft Clarity, Hotjar, LinkedIn Insight Tag, TikTok Pixel, YouTube and Vimeo. Load cookieowl first with data-auto-block and their scripts are held even when a vendor snippet injects them, and video embeds become a click to load placeholder.

What gets stored

A small, honest record

cookieowl keeps one first party cookie with the choice and nothing else. No server, no fingerprint, no third party. Send the same record to your own log with onConsent if you need proof of consent.

Fields of a consent record
FieldWhat it holds
idRandom consent id, kept across updates on the same device
versionThe policy version the visitor agreed to
timestampWhen the choice was made, ISO 8601
expiresWhen the banner asks again (12 months by default)
methodaccept-all, reject-all, custom or signal
categoriesEvery category and whether it was accepted
lang and pageThe banner language and the path where the choice was made

FAQ

Questions people ask about cookie consent

Not finding yours? Open an issue on GitHub.

Does cookieowl really block Google Analytics before consent?

Yes. Scripts you mark with type="text/plain" and data-cookieowl="analytics" are not executed by the browser at all, so gtag.js is never requested until analytics is accepted. With data-auto-block the gtag.js and Tag Manager URLs are also caught when they are inserted by a snippet. If you use Consent Mode advanced instead, Google tags load and send cookieless pings, which you choose explicitly.

Is cookieowl free for commercial sites?

Yes. It is MIT licensed, has no paid tier, no page view limits, no account and no server. You load one file from jsDelivr or install it from npm and host it yourself.

Does it support Google Consent Mode v2?

Yes. With consentMode on, cookieowl sets ad_storage, ad_user_data, ad_personalization and analytics_storage to denied with wait_for_update before any tag runs, supports region specific defaults, and sends a consent update on every choice, including on later visits.

Is a cookie banner enough to comply with LGPD or GDPR?

No banner alone makes a site compliant. cookieowl handles the technical part: prior blocking, granular and free choice, withdrawal as easy as consent, versioned records and an audit hook. You still need an accurate privacy policy, a legal basis for each processing and a review by your own counsel. This site is not legal advice.

Where is the consent stored and for how long?

In a first party cookie named cookieowl (or localStorage if you prefer) holding the categories, a random consent id, the policy version, a timestamp and an expiry date. The default lifetime is 12 months, after which the banner asks again. Changing your policy version asks everyone again right away.

How do visitors change their mind?

A small floating owl button reopens the preferences on every page, and any element with data-cookieowl-open does the same, for example a Cookie settings link in your footer. Withdrawing erases the cookies of that category and reloads the page when its scripts already ran.

Does it work with React, Vue, Next.js, Angular and WordPress?

Yes. The core is framework agnostic. cookieowl/react adds a useConsent hook and a ConsentGate component, cookieowl/vue adds a plugin, a composable and the same gate, and for WordPress you add the script tag in the theme head. Each one has a guide.

Will it slow my site down?

The script is about 15 kB gzip with five languages, the stylesheet and the blocker included, and it has no dependencies. It must load synchronously in the head to block trackers, which costs a few milliseconds, and it makes no network requests of its own.

What happens with Global Privacy Control?

When a browser sends Global Privacy Control, cookieowl records a refusal of optional categories, does not show the banner and notes the signal in the preferences, where the visitor can still opt in. Do Not Track can be honored the same way with respectDNT.

Can I keep a consent log for audits?

Yes. Pass onConsent(record) and send each record to your own endpoint. Every record has a random id, the policy version, the timestamp, the method (accept all, reject all, custom or signal), the categories, the language and the page.
